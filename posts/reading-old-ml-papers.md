*A short note on why the 1990s literature is underrated.*

Most reading lists in machine learning start around 2012. That's a shame, because the older literature is full of ideas that were correct but early — starved of compute, waiting for hardware to catch up.

Reading old papers teaches you two things the modern literature can't:

1. **Which ideas are load-bearing.** When you see backprop, mixture models, or attention-like mechanisms invented three separate times under different names, you learn what the field keeps rediscovering because it's actually fundamental.
2. **How claims age.** Every era's papers contain confident predictions. Checking them against thirty years of hindsight is the best calibration training available.

Start with anything by Schmidhuber's lab from the 90s, the original MDL papers, and the neural network winter retrospectives. Read them like a time traveler, not a historian.
